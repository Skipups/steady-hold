Reading the screen

Time flows right to left, like a rhythm game. Targets scroll in from the right so you can see what’s coming. The purple line is your squeeze and the dot is “now”. Coral = squeeze into this zone. Sage = let go completely. 100% = your calibrated max standing.

Beeps and voice cues (“Squeeze”, “Let go”, “Cough!”) mean you can stand with the phone on a shelf at eye level instead of looking down. Looking down tips the pelvis.

Why standing is the default

Gravity and the weight of your organs sit on the pelvic floor when you’re upright. That’s when leaks happen, and a squeeze is harder to produce than lying down. So:

  • Separate calibration per position. Your standing max is lower than lying, so 100% always means your standing max. Standing scores are never mixed with lying ones.
  • Standing levels L1–L5 make any game harder in real-life ways: stand still → weight shifts → mini squats (lift as you rise) → march in place → holding baby or a weight. The cue shows at the bottom of the screen.
  • Standing milestones in Progress come from the postnatal return-to-running guideline: 10 fast flicks, 8–12 near-max 6–8 s holds, and a 60 s hold at 30–50%, all standing.
  • Cough Shield is done standing, because that’s where cough and sneeze leaks happen.

Today’s plan: what to play, and how often

Each time you open the app, the Today’s session card shows what to do and Start session runs the games in order. That’s one ~7-minute session a day, aiming for 4–5 days a week. The dots show your days this week. This matches the research: about 8–12 strong holds in three series, 3–4 times a week, builds strength, and sticking with it for 3–5 months matters more than doing a lot.

Order inside a session: warm-up → strength while you’re fresh → endurance under load → a skill.

Form reminders

Under the main instruction, a small coaching line rotates through the cues from the research. These can also be spoken. They're timed so they never talk over “Squeeze” or “Let go”.

  • Pelvic floor first: squeeze & lift, then add belly, breath or movement. Bladder-neck lift only happens when the pelvic floor effort is high relative to belly pressure.
  • Lower belly light, about 20%. Don’t brace. A hard deep-ab contraction pushed the pelvic floor down in about 30% of women in one study.
  • Breathe out softly, like blowing out candles. A gentle out-breath lifted the pelvic floor best; forceful exhaling didn’t.
  • Keep breathing. Never hold your breath, which builds pressure downward.
  • Breathe in and let go. The pelvic floor naturally drops and widens on the in-breath.
  • In Squeeze & Move: squeeze first, then exhale as you lift into the bridge or lift a foot.

Turn them off (or turn off just the spoken ones) in Setup & extras.

The form check (straining guard)

A correct squeeze lifts up and in. Bearing down pushes out, and both raise pressure. In Setup you do one gentle bear-down and one squeeze. The app learns how the pressure splits between the Perifit’s two sensors for each. After that, any rep that looks more like the bear-down gets a “check your lift” flag and no points, so the games never reward the wrong movement. If your two signatures are too similar to tell apart, the check stays off and the app tells you.

How progress is tracked

  • Every session is saved with date, position and standing level, so trends only compare like with like.
  • Each game has one headline number with a sparkline: Hold score, Power peak, Flicks landed, Elevator accuracy, Wave accuracy, Melt release time, Breath sync and Cough Shield. Results also show ▲/▼ vs your last session.
  • Auto-progression: targets move up only after a clean set (≥ 80, no bearing down, relaxed between reps), and move down if a set scored under 50.
  • Monthly Check-up gives a report card: strength, hold time, repeat holds, quick flicks, let-go time, Cough Shield and leak score.
  • Leak score (0–12, your own symptom questions) is the real measure. A drop of 3+ points that lasts is real improvement. Ticking a warning symptom shows a “see a pelvic floor PT” note.
  • Warning signs it watches: resting level creeping up, slow or missed releases, or lots of bear-down flags. These point to tension, not weakness, so the advice is Melt and a PT visit, not more squeezing.

Try “Bears down instead of lifting” or “Slow to relax” to see the flags those habits trigger.
